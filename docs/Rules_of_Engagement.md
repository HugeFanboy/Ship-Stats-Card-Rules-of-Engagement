The system uses multiple d12s. The default target number (TN) is 9; a success on the d12 means rolling 9 or higher; some effects or actions can raise or lower the TN.

This system works best for two or three combatants in total. With more combatants, the (abstract) tactical map becomes more or less useless. It is therefore suggested, for large fleet battles, to use the optional rules _Fleets and Armadas_.

This ruleset is meant to be a sort of mini-game to play out instead of a group check or Montage Test or the like; in most cases, for most groups, when wanting to portray ship-to-ship combat, **it is better to have the players roll to overcome a series of obstacles, rather than to switch to this random mini-game.**

If the GM wants extra complexity for the fun of it, by all means, use as much of this system as you dare.
# Basic Rules
Each combatant (i.e., each ship) can take up to three actions per round, assigned to up to three different positions. Each position has access to a specific set of actions. 

You cannot use the same action more than once in a single round, except for the _Maneuver_ action.

At the start of a battle (beginning of the encounter) the GM announces the opponents (including the hero ship) and briefly establishes some details: ship class, name, any other noticeable factor. The GM then places tokens on the map for enemy ships. The GM can either choose each ship’s starting position or roll for it: roll 1d4 for range (1 = Short, 4 = Extreme) and 1d8 for sector (result = sector number).

> [!NOTE]
> The term _“hero ship”_ is used to mean the vessel the players control, even if said players are dastardly scoundrels.

## Offense
### Volley
This represents the number of broadside weapons, the basic armament, your ship has available. The type of weapon is more or less irrelevant. Mundane ballistae or cannons or—depending on your setting—magic guns or photonic blasters are all, mechanically, the same: **Volley**.

> [!NOTE]
> It’s worth mentioning that **Volley** technically represents the weapons on only one side of the ship; see [[#Range and Weapon Facing]] for more details.

When attacking with your **Volley**, you expend **Energy** dice up to the number of weapons available: your **Volley** stat.

Ammunition (ammo) or charge for your **Volley** weapons is always assumed to be plentiful. By default, each weapon deals 1 point of damage. Modifications to the **Volley** can increase this damage or add a special effect—for instance, incendiary rounds. See _[[#Modifications]]_ for further details on options for ammo or weapons.
### Prime
**Prime** weaponry is something else. You can only have one **Prime** weapon—unless you have the _Weapons platform_ modification (see _[[#Modifications]]_). The **Prime** stat represents the number of ammo or charge for the weapon you have. In other words, the **Prime** stat is the maximum number of times this weapon can be used in a battle.

In general, the **Prime** weapon deals 5 damage if at least one of the attack dice remains after the enemy’s defense.
## Defense
There are two ways to defend against attacks: evasion (or dodging) and armour (or shielding).
### Evasion
To evade, roll up to your ship’s **Evasion** dice. **Evasion** dice are independent from the **Energy** pool. Each success cancels one incoming hit—one **Evasion** success cancels one enemy _Attack_ dice. The **Evasion** number typically corelates to the **Maneuverability** stat, often both being equal before modifications.
### Armour
You may add your full **Armour** value to the Evasion roll. If **Armour** is depleted, you can only rely on **Evasion**.

**Armour** automatically cancels a number of attacks equal to the its value, decrementing that value after the attack is resolved.

**Armour** can be repaired or replenished in dry dock.

> The (hero) pirate ship—pardon me—the _privateer_ frigate The _Jolly Drunk_ is being attacked by a navy vessel. The enemy fires their main guns (8 Volley); the _Jolly Drunk_ needs to defend against 8 dice.
>  
> The players choose to use their Armour (4) and make a hard maneuver (4 Evasion). The _Jolly Drunk_ thus rolls 4d12 and they get 2 successes. The ship only takes 2 points of damage (damage received = 8 attack − 4 Armour − 2 Evasion successes = 2 damage).
> 
> The players then decrement their Armour to 3 (4 Armour − 1 = 3 Armour).
## Mobility and Ranges
Refer to the graphs below. The ranges and movements are abstract. By design, the exact measurements are not relevant to the mechanics.
![[TacticalGraphWhiteOnBlack.webp]]
![[TacticalGraphBlackOnWhite.webp]]
In the graphs, The central arrow shows your ship and its heading; its size is exaggerated to represent Immediate range. Immediate range means walking or jumping distance. Depending on the context or setting, it might mean larger distances in the order of a few or even several dozen metres. The other ranges are progressively larger, of course.

The diagrams are velocity‑relative: they show the position of opponents relative to one another while both are in motion. Because the map is relative, a 2D graph is sufficient to represent up to three combatants (including the hero ship) even for three‑dimensional movement.

Because of these simplifications, the ship can “move” in any direction. Moving in (or closing distance) and moving out (or making distance) keep you in the same numbered sector but change the range by one step. For instance, if an opponent is at Medium range and you move in, the opponent (represented by a token of your choice on the tactical graph) will then appear at Short range. If you moved out instead, the opponent would appear at Long range within the same slice—called sector—of the circle graph.
### Movement in Battle
You may perform up to two maneuvers as part of a single action. You can use the same maneuver twice in one round. You may split the action across other actions (e.g., perform a maneuver, take another action, then perform a second maneuver).

You can move in **_or_** move out **_or_** you can shift sector as one maneuver. In other words, you can change the distance to the enemy and you can rotate the ship in one direction as part of the same action. “Diagonal movement” therefore is achieved by using both maneuvers.

> The _Jolly Drunk_ takes the *Maneuver* action. The crew decides to move in to reach their weapons’ effective range, attack, and then retreat by moving out. This sequence involves two actions: one maneuver to move in, one maneuver to move out (one action) and one action to attack.

When tracking two opponents beside the hero ship, you need to consider multiple sectors. Adjacent cells within the same range circle are classified as Short range from one another, while two cells apart are at Medium range. Additionally, the distance between range circles is also treated as short range. This means that “diagonally” adjacent cells are two range steps apart, or Medium range.
### Maneuverability
The **Maneuverability** stat represents the distance the ship can travel during a complete movement _action_. In other words, all maneuvers combined must not exceed this number.

You can move more than one range step or one sector in a single maneuver, as long as it stays within your **Maneuverability** limit.

When controlling an NPC enemy ship, they can only move in or move out by at most half of their _[[#Maneuverability]]_ (rounded down) range steps and they cannot shift sector on their own—since shifting sector represents turning the hero ship, not moving in that direction.
### Initiative
At the start of each round, each combatant rolls their initiative dice. Refer to the classification table below. This roll is not an action. The lower roll acts first; tied rolls are re-rolled between the tied parties until the tie is broken.

| *Ship Class* | *Initiative Dice* |
| ------------ | ----------------- |
| Battleship   | 2d12              |
| Cruiser      | 2d10              |
| Frigate      | 2d8               |
| Corvette     | 2d6               |
| Destroyer    | 2d4               |
Most hero ships will be classified as Destroyers or Corvettes, maybe a Frigate if you want a larger NPC crew aboard. It’s uncommon for PCs to command a Cruiser, Battleship (or equivalents) for long stretches—maybe for a single special battle. That said, the rules don’t *forbid* the heroes from piloting a Battleship with a three‑person crew. Go wild.

Combatants may take up to three actions in any order during their turn. If a combatant cannot take any action, they simply lose their turn and play passes to the next combatant in initiative.
### Range and Weapon Facing
Using directional weapons, your ship will usually have axial symmetry, which is the basis for this system. The **Volley** section of the ship’s stat card indicates the number of weapons on one side of the ship.

Default weapons (that is, unmodified weapons) can target an arc spanning three sectors: the starboard side weapons can fire at targets in sectors 2, 3, or 4, while the port side weapons can engage targets in sectors 6, 7, or 8. This means you cannot fire towards the stern or bow—also known as aft and fore—in sectors 1 and 5, respectively.

Default weapons have an effective range of Medium. This means there are no penalties for firing at that distance or closer. However, if you fire from a further distance, the target number (TN) for the enemy’s Evasion roll decreases by 2 for each additional range step. For example, if you fire at a ship positioned at Long range while your weapons have a Medium effective range, the TN for their Evasion roll will be 7.

> [!NOTE]
> You’re encouraged to use the graph and movement rules for tactical positioning to enhance your combat experience. This suggests your weapons occupy a physical space on your ship and face specific directions. However, if you prefer a simpler experience, you *can* mostly disregard the graph and focus solely on ranges, using just one sector.

In the event you have enemies on both sides of the ship, remember you can use the _Attack_ action twice, but only once per ship side. You can fire your starboard weapons (first action) and then fire your port weapons (second action)—or vice versa, of course.

**Prime weapons**, unless stated otherwise, are oriented towards the stern and bow (sectors 1 and 5), but you can only fire one direction at a time, just like standard weapons. This is a simplification designed to keep the stats and calculations straightforward.
### Ramming
Yes, ramming. Although it’s not a recommended tactic or indeed a useful one in most cases, it can be fun and dramatic to “CHARGE!” the enemy or to have a player call out: “Prepare for ramming speed!”

Therefore, you can attempt to ram and opponent if they are directly in front of your ship (in sector 1). In order to successfully ram, announce your intention and Commit dice for the ramming, up to your **Maneuverability**; the ramming will be considered a variant of the _Attack_ action upon contact.

When attacking in this way (i.e., at the moment of contact), the opponent will evade as usual, removing a number of ramming dice equal to their Evasion roll successes. Each remaining dice deals 1 damage to the enemy and 1 to your own ship. After resolving that damage, roll the remaining committed dice (Expend them), with the TN increased by 1 for each range step above Short range. Specifically, Short range ramming has a TN of 9 (standard); Medium range has a TN of 10; Long range has a TN of 11; Extreme range has a TN of 12. Each success deals an additional 5 points of damage to the enemy. If your ship’s **Structural Integrity** reaches 0 from the initial impact, it is destroyed resulting in no further damage to the enemy.

> The _Jolly Drunk_ is badly damaged. In desperation, its crew decides to ram the enemy navy ship staring them down straight ahead, to bow. They loudly declare, “All speed ahead!”
> They are at Long range now; they start the round by committing all of their Maneuverability dice to the ramming, making a pool of 4 dice. The enemy ship notices that and, in the next round, they move out to Extreme range. The _Jolly Drunk_’s crew decide to move closer before attacking; they maneuver into Long range again. The third round, the crew moves in again; the enemy does not move. The fourth round, the navy ship moves first and closes the gap to fire their Prime weapons; the _Jolly Drunk_ is now at Short range and the crew decides to commit one more round of dice; the ramming pool is now 8 dice. The fifth round, the _Jolly Drunk_ manages to act first: they ram. The navy ship evades against 8 dice, managing 3 successes; 5 ramming dice remain. The crew of the _Jolly Drunk_ roll their ramming dice and get 4 successes. They deal a total of 25 damage to the enemy (5 ramming dice + 4 successes × 5), and they take 5 damage to their own ship, rendering them barely capable of floating, let alone seaworthy.
### Boarding
If you want to board the enemy ship, it must be within Short range and a special *Boarding* 
action will be used (variant of the _Attack_ action). Conversely, you can be boarded under the same conditions.

When boarding, move some or all of your characters to the boarded ship. You then have two options:
1. You can transition to character combat (according to your setting and normal ruleset).
2. The boarded ship capitulates, ending any ongoing effect or damage over time and the crew of the boarded ship is effectively disarmed.

Either way, the ship-to-ship combat that this ruleset represents should end.
## Power
### Energy
**Energy** is represented by a pool of d12s that refreshes each round. Any **Energy** unused by the end of the round is lost. The amount of **Energy** you have is based on the ship’s **Systems**. If the **Systems** value decreases during battle, your **Energy** will decrease as well; this takes effect on your next turn.

Dice that have been committed won’t refresh and return to the pool until they’ve fulfilled that commitment. You may have more dice committed than your maximum **Energy**.

The default amount of **Energy** your ship has available is equal to 3 times your **Systems**.
### Systems
This number reflects the ship’s overall worthiness. If it reaches 0, the ship can no longer function. In this case, the ship isn’t destroyed, but it’s severely damaged and would need major repairs to be operational again.
## Modifications
Modifications can be anything and everything. Nearly every rule can be tweaked, amended, or completely rewritten by modifications (or mods). All ships have 8 maximum modifications, but the size, shape, or weight of the mod is irrelevant (unless you want it to be). You can only have one mod per ship stat. You can only modify your **Volley** one way, you can only modify your **Energy** output in a certain way, and so on. For instance, if you have a mod for your **Volley** that increases damage, you cannot also have a mod that extends the range of the **Volley**.

None of these modifications are proscriptive. Feel free to come up with new mods or to change the ones offered here. Always discuss with your group which mods they would like and how they want them—ergo, whether they want to buy it, build it, steal it, or anything else. It has no mechanical impact, but it can be an interesting goal to work towards.

## Damage
When you receive damage, you choose to either apply points of damage to the **Structural Integrity** of the ship or to forgo damage to instead decrement the **Systems** value. If a section or a system has been targeted, however, you _must_ apply the effect to the appropriate section.

If the damage taken reduces the **Structural Integrity** to 0, the ship is destroyed. Furthermore, whenever the ship takes 10 points of damage or more in a single salvo, roll on the Effects Table (see _[[#Effects Table]]_ below). If a system has been targeted, you must roll on the Effects Table and decrement the **Systems**.
